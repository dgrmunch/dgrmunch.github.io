Profile

Diego González Rodríguez is a professor at Universidad Camilo José Cela (Madrid) and, since 2021, a collaborating researcher at the Department of Neuromedicine and Movement Science of the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU). His work lies at the intersection of philosophy of mind, cognitive science and information processing in complex systems.

His current research focuses on cognition from an ethno-phenomenological approach, in dialogue with Indian philosophy, and on the relevance of the Śaiva-Śākta traditions to contemporary debates on consciousness and artificial systems. He holds a PhD from Universidad Carlos III de Madrid and has been a visiting researcher at the University of Toronto, Tallinn University of Technology and the University of North Carolina.

Research

Indian philosophy and cognitive science
The relevance of Indian philosophy, especially the Śaiva-Śākta traditions, to contemporary cognitive science and the study of complex systems.
Ethno-phenomenology
Phenomenological and ethnographic methods for the study of first-person experience and non-ordinary states of consciousness.
Distributed cognition
Bio-inspired computing, complex systems and distributed cognition from a processual and relational perspective.
Language and systems
Computational linguistics and self-organized linguistic systems.
